Emergency Awareness Course

🚑 First Response

Emergency Actions in Dangerous Situations — learn how to recognize urgent conditions, protect the scene, request help, and provide basic support until trained responders arrive.

Important: This course is for general awareness only. In a real emergency, call 911 in the United States and follow the dispatcher’s instructions. Formal CPR and first-aid certification is strongly recommended.
01
🛡️

Check Scene Safety

Before approaching, look for traffic, fire, electricity, chemicals, violence, unstable structures, or other hazards.

Do not become a second injured person.
02
📞

Call Emergency Services

Call 911 for someone who is unresponsive, has difficulty breathing, shows signs of stroke, has life-threatening bleeding, or has another serious emergency.

Give the location and follow the dispatcher’s instructions.
03
❤️

Sudden Adult Collapse

For a teen or adult who suddenly collapses and is not responding:

  • Call 911 or send someone to call.
  • Push hard and fast in the center of the chest.
  • Use an AED when available.
Hands-only CPR is intended for sudden teen or adult collapse.
04
🫁

Choking

A person with severe choking may be unable to speak, cough effectively, or breathe.

  • Call 911 or have someone call.
  • Provide trained choking care.
  • Continue until the object clears or help arrives.
Infant choking care is different from adult care.
05
🩸

Severe Bleeding

Life-threatening bleeding requires immediate action.

  • Call 911.
  • Apply firm, direct pressure to the wound.
  • Use protective gloves when available.
  • Use a tourniquet only when appropriate and trained.
Maintain pressure until emergency help takes over.
06
🔥

Burns

First stop the burning process and make sure the area is safe.

  • Move away from the heat source.
  • Cool a thermal burn with clean, cool running water.
  • Do not apply ice directly to the burn.
  • Seek emergency help for serious burns.
Do not remove material that is stuck to the skin.
07
⚡

Seizure

During a seizure, focus on protecting the person from injury.

  • Clear dangerous objects from the area.
  • Do not restrain the person.
  • Do not place anything in their mouth.
  • Time the seizure and call for help when needed.
Stay nearby and monitor breathing after movement stops.
08
🧠

Recognize a Stroke

Remember F.A.S.T.:

  • Face: Is one side drooping?
  • Arms: Does one arm drift downward?
  • Speech: Is speech slurred or unusual?
  • Time: Call 911 immediately.
Note when the symptoms first appeared.
09
☠️

Possible Poisoning

Do not guess at treatment or automatically make the person vomit.

  • Call Poison Control in the U.S.: 1-800-222-1222.
  • Keep the container or product information nearby.
  • Call 911 for collapse, seizure, breathing trouble, or inability to wake.
Poisoning instructions depend on the specific substance.

🚨 When in Doubt, Call for Help

Protect yourself, call emergency services, follow dispatcher instructions, and provide only the care you are trained and able to perform safely.
